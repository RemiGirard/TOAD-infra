Your app, from code to a public link

You built something on your computer. To let other people use it, it needs an address, somewhere to run, and a way to reach the right app securely. TOAD prepares those parts in your own cloud account, from infrastructure and configuration defined in code. You or your coding agent use the same open-source CLI to deploy and operate the platform. You keep control of the files, account access, costs and responsibilities.

Read it in two directions. The green arrows show you shipping the app: prepare the cloud once, publish a package, then deploy it. The blue arrows show a visitor reaching that same app. The dotted DNS arrows only find the address. The purple arrows bring observations back to you and your agent.

Your computer can be off after deployment: the app runs on the rented servers. The visual quickstart explains each step, and the complete Infomaniak example supplies the commands.

Not sure you want to operate a cloud platform? Compare the responsibilities first. You can also preview the example locally without buying cloud resources.

Your platform stays in your hands

  • Your account: cloud resources belong to your provider project. You choose access and capacity and pay the provider directly.
  • Your definitions: Heat templates describe infrastructure, Ansible playbooks configure the servers, and Compose/app manifests describe workloads. They are files you can inspect, keep in Git and change.
  • Your workflow: TOAD runs from your operator machine or automation and coordinates provisioning, releases and diagnostics. Your app source can stay in its own repository, using a registry you choose.
  • Your ability to adapt: the current TOAD distribution is MIT licensed. You can inspect, fork and modify it while retaining the required notices. See the license and its scope.

The cloud hardware is rented, and your chosen providers charge for their services. You still operate the platform and plan data storage and recovery. Infomaniak is the verified path; other OpenStack providers require adaptation.

Follow one visit to your app

This is a teaching map of the maintained TOAD setup, not a reading of your account or a health report. The public homepage lets you select each part for an explanation. No account or credentials are needed to explore it. It also compares a one-server IPv6 design with the current three-server workflow. The one-server TOAD profile still needs implementation and validation; provider support alone does not make a TOAD deployment recipe ready. The complete Infomaniak example connects these concepts to concrete names, reserved example IPs and the actual commands.

  1. A visitor opens my-app.platform.example.com.
  2. Their browser asks DNS for the name's address. DNS returns the public load-balancer address; it does not carry the website traffic.
  3. The browser connects to that address over HTTPS. The load balancer passes the connection through the servers' Swarm network to Traefik.
  4. Traefik handles the encrypted browser connection and routes the request to the right running app. Encryption ends here in this setup; the diagram does not claim encrypted backend connections.
  5. The app returns the page or API result. It accesses a database or files only if your app and its deployment have been configured to use them.

The names, in ordinary language

Term Think of it as What you need to decide
Domain The name you control, such as example.com. An app can use a name below it, such as my-app.platform.example.com. Which name to use and who maintains its registration. It is separate from renting servers.
DNS A lookup that finds the public address for a name. Which zone TOAD manages. Its first setup needs a one-time child-zone delegation; your agent can explain the exact records.
Public cloud Computers, networking and storage rented through your provider account. OpenStack is the API family TOAD uses to manage resources. Owner, region, capacity, costs and access. Infomaniak is TOAD's verified path; other providers need adaptation and validation.
Server / VM One rented computer that runs part of your platform. The maintained profile uses three. Three managers protect coordination after one manager loss, not every app or dataset.
Load balancer The platform's public front door, distributing connections to its servers. Resource costs and routing. Octavia passes TCP connections through in this setup.
HTTPS / TLS Encryption for the connection from a visitor's browser to your HTTPS gateway. “SSL” is the older name people often use. Your domain and certificate setup. HTTPS does not add app login or make insecure app code safe.
Traefik The gateway that handles HTTPS certificates and selects the app for a hostname or path. TOAD runs one replica with a local certificate store; ingress has an availability limit.
Image / container The packaged app and runtime dependencies / a running copy of that package. Your app's build, port and configuration. Your agent can prepare these from your source. A running copy is also called a replica.
Registry A place to publish and retrieve app images. Which registry and access policy to use. TOAD pins the exact image digest so a release identifies specific content.
Volume / backup Files kept beyond a container's lifetime / an independent recoverable copy of data. A database is not supplied automatically. Local volumes stay on one chosen node; offsite backup and a tested restore need explicit setup.

What you have after each stage

Stage The result you should be able to see What has not happened yet
Account and project access An owned project, dedicated OpenStack access and local private configuration. Creating access does not provision your platform. A domain is a separate prerequisite.
Platform applied and verified Three managers, public load balancer, DNS routing, HTTPS gateway and monitoring configured; a platform verification result. Your own app has not shipped. Verification is evidence at a point in time.
App released and diagnosed Your app URL, intended versus running image, ready replicas and declared HTTPS check results. Passing endpoint checks does not prove every feature works or that data is recoverable.
Operations prepared A private view of nodes, releases, checks and agent operation stages; agreed maintenance and recovery procedures. An open dashboard does not prove alerts reached someone. Backups need independent copies and restore validation.

Use the account guide, then the cloud-first quickstart. Reuse an existing verified platform when you have one; each new app does not need another three-server platform.

See your own infrastructure, privately

After setup, ask your agent:

Open TOAD's private operation view for the selected context and my app. Explain the observed servers, running app copies and HTTPS checks in plain language. Show the capture time and anything unknown. Distinguish the route illustration from collected health evidence.

The operation view draws a routing model and shows the actual observed servers and services for the selected context/app. It does not discover a complete DNS, load-balancer, container-placement or data map. Those parts must not turn green just because the illustration has arrows.

The generated HTML stays on your operator machine. With the local collector running in --watch mode it refreshes; observations expire after one minute. It contains private details and should not be published with the project site.

Admin dashboards are a different surface: they use the public HTTPS gateway with an additional client-certificate requirement, called mTLS. This is not the same thing as app-user authentication or a VPN-only network.

Choose your level of responsibility

There is no universal winner. This compares the normal workflows, not prices, every feature or every paid plan. Provider documentation was reviewed on 28 October 2026; check current limits and costs for your app.

Approach Where your app runs What is convenient What you still operate or decide
Hosted app platform, such as Render or Railway On the provider's managed deployment platform. Connect a repository or image and configure a service. Render supplies a public service URL and managed TLS; Railway builds/deploys services from source or images. App behavior, configuration, data policy, access and billing. Supported runtimes, limits and deployment models follow the provider.
Platform on your servers, such as Coolify On servers you provide and control. Dashboard/API workflows for builds, deployments, domains and service operations. Your servers, workload security, data and backups. Self-hosting also means running the Coolify control plane; Coolify Cloud manages that control plane separately.
Your cloud defined in Git, with TOAD In your OpenStack-based cloud project; currently verified on Infomaniak. Provision the platform and use the same local CLI for human or agent delivery, diagnosis and evidence. Cloud account, domain, server/platform upkeep, capacity, app security, costs, notifications, backups and tested recovery.

Sources: Render web services, Railway's building blocks, Coolify's model and Coolify Cloud responsibilities.
